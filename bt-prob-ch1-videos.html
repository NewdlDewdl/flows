    Key points

    • Conditional probability revises a model on partial news: once the outcome is known to lie in B, the updated chance that it also lies in A is P(A | B).

    Conditional probabilities obey all three axioms

    0:00–7:44YouTube app

    Checks nonnegativity, P(Ω | B) = 1, P(B | B) = 1 (B as the new universe) and additivity for two disjoint events, the same derivation as the page, then says every formula for probabilities carries over to conditional ones.

    Key points

    • P(A | B) requires P(B) > 0; given a zero-probability event it is undefined. It is the share of B's probability that sits on outcomes also in A.
    • For a fixed B, conditional probabilities obey all three axioms: nonnegativity, P(Ω | B) = 1, and additivity for disjoint A1, A2 (and countably many).
    • So every rule for probability laws holds given B: P(A ∪ C) ≤ P(A) + P(C) turns into P(A ∪ C | B) ≤ P(A | B) + P(C | B).
    • Since P(B | B) = 1, all conditional probability sits on B: drop the outcomes outside B and treat B as the new universe.

    Answer. 7/12 (about 0.583)

    Conditional probabilities given 'late' form a probability law, so inclusion-exclusion carries over: 1/2 + 1/3 - 1/4 = 7/12.

    Tsitsiklis introduces the basic counting principle by getting dressed in stages: 4 shirts then 3 ties give 4 x 3 = 12 shirt-tie pairs, and 2 jackets double that to 24, the product of the numbers of options at the stages. The shirt-then-tie step is exactly the page's two-stage count of m x n ordered pairs.

    Key points

    • Many probabilities come down to counting. With finitely many equally likely outcomes, P(A) = (number of outcomes in A)/(number of outcomes in Ω).
    • When every outcome in A has the same known probability p, P(A) = p times the number of outcomes in A; k heads in n tosses is one such case.
    • Counting sounds easy but often gets subtle; much of combinatorics is devoted to it.
    • Two-stage counting: m results at stage one and n at stage two give mn ordered pairs (ai, bj); a tree lays out the stages.
